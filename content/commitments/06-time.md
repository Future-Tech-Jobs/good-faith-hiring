## Why it matters

A five-stage process with a weekend-long task asks a lot of someone who has a job, children or a disability. A pile of copy-paste applications asks a lot of whoever reads them. Each side's shortcuts cost the other side time.

## For employers

Tell people what the process looks like before they start. "A 30-minute call, a 90-minute technical interview, then a final chat, all within three weeks" is enough.

Keep unpaid take-home tasks to 3 hours at most. If the task needs longer, pay for it. A task should test the must-haves, not deliver free work.

Offer adjustments at every stage, and make it easy to ask. Examples include extra time, questions in writing in advance, a different format for a task, or a video call instead of travelling. Say how to ask in your first reply (see [commitment 7](/keeping-in-touch/)).

## For candidates

Apply for fewer jobs, and make each one count. Read the whole advert, check the first four commitments, and say in your application why you fit this role.

AI and assistive tools can help you write. Spelling tools, dictation and AI drafting are all fine. What you say must be true, and it should be about this job, not every job.

## Questions

**Is sending lots of applications against the standard?** The number isn't the problem. Sending the same application to roles you haven't read is.

**Can an employer reject me for using AI?** This standard doesn't ask employers to. It asks that what you send is true and written for the role.
